# Government-place classification codebook, pilot v0.2

## Population and sampling frame

The target population for place classification is the set of unique Google place
listings that represented a U.S. location when the UCSD Google Local metadata
snapshot was assembled. The operational frame is every unique nonblank
`gmap_id` in the 50 state partitions and District of Columbia partition.
`Other` is excluded from the primary frame unless a U.S. state can be resolved.

Official rosters extend candidate discovery but do not redefine the population.
Current sources, including the USGS National Structures Dataset, are validation
and discovery evidence rather than a time-aligned 2021 denominator.

## Unit

One Google place listing identified by gmap_id. A listing is not automatically a
physical facility: one facility can have multiple listings, and one building can
house several facilities.

## Core decision

A core government place must satisfy all three conditions:

1. the listing represents a government agency or a facility delivering a delegated
   public administrative service;
2. members of the public can have a qualifying encounter with that facility;
3. a review can plausibly describe that encounter rather than only a landmark,
   geographic area, museum, memorial, or unrelated colocated business.

## Closed analytic-tier labels

### core

- **Definition:** A government or delegated public-service listing at which a
  person can complete, contest, or receive an administrative transaction.
- **Includes:** DMV offices, benefit offices, tax-assistance offices, clerks,
  courts, permit offices, and staffed postal counters.
- **Excludes:** emergency response, parks, schools, hospitals, unattended
  infrastructure, and private regulated businesses.
- **Positive examples:** “County Clerk and Recorder”; “Social Security Field
  Office”; “Department of Motor Vehicles.”
- **Negative examples:** “Volunteer Fire Department”; “H&R Block”; “City Park.”

### secondary

- **Definition:** A public-sector or delegated-service listing with a qualifying
  encounter generated by a substantively distinct observation process.
- **Includes:** police, fire/EMS, corrections, public libraries, ranger stations,
  and staffed public visitor centers.
- **Excludes:** ordinary administrative transactions and private providers whose
  public role is unverified.
- **Positive examples:** “Municipal Police Department”; “Central Public Library”;
  “County Fire Station 4.”
- **Negative examples:** “County Clerk”; “Police Museum”; “Private Security.”

### separate_sector

- **Definition:** A public institution that may deliver public service but must
  be modeled as its own sector rather than pooled with administrative offices.
- **Includes:** public schools, public universities, public hospitals, transit
  agencies, public housing, and public utilities after ownership validation.
- **Excludes:** private schools and hospitals, and ordinary administrative
  offices located on a campus.
- **Positive examples:** “State University”; “County General Hospital”; “Public
  School District High School.”
- **Negative examples:** “Private Academy”; “Urgent Care LLC”; “DMV Office.”

### exclude

- **Definition:** The listing does not represent an eligible public-sector place
  or a reviewable public-service encounter.
- **Includes:** private regulated firms, campaigns, museums or memorials,
  landmarks, unattended infrastructure, and unrelated colocated businesses.
- **Excludes:** difficult or unpleasant public encounters, which remain eligible,
  and uncertain ownership cases, which receive `uncertain`.
- **Positive examples:** “UPS Store”; “Police History Museum”; “City Hall Cafe.”
- **Negative examples:** “U.S. Post Office”; “County Jail”; “Public Library.”

### uncertain

- **Definition:** Available evidence cannot distinguish between an eligible place
  and an exclusion without additional official or human research.
- **Includes:** ambiguous names, unknown ownership, duplicate listings, shared
  addresses, and automated many-to-one matches.
- **Excludes:** clearly eligible or clearly private listings.
- **Positive examples:** “Community Service Center” with no owner; a hospital
  with unknown ownership; two Google IDs for one official facility.
- **Negative examples:** exact DMV match; named private tax preparer.

## Fields

### government_level

- federal
- state
- county
- municipal
- special_district
- mixed
- uncertain

### ownership_status

- public
- contracted_public_service
- quasi_public
- private_regulated_only
- uncertain

Private entities are not treated as government merely because they are regulated.

### encounter_mode

- transaction: license, benefit, filing, mailing, permit, record, payment
- adjudication: court, hearing, appeal, enforcement decision
- emergency: police, fire, emergency response
- community: library, community program, public meeting
- amenity: park, recreation, visitor attraction
- nonpublic: administrative building with no public-facing service

### public_facing

- yes
- no
- uncertain

### analytic_tier

- core: clear administrative or transactional service
- secondary: politically important but distinct observation process
- separate_sector: public institution requiring its own sector-specific model
- exclude: no qualifying public-service encounter for this study
- uncertain: requires adjudication

## Common exclusions

- UPS, FedEx, private mailbox, shipping, and postal-annex stores;
- driving schools, private tag agents, and car dealers;
- tax preparers, accountants, and title companies;
- nonprofit or private providers absent a separately defined delegated-service
  research question;
- police/fire museums, memorials, and historic markers;
- collection boxes and unattended infrastructure;
- neighborhoods, cities, oceans, and other geographic features;
- restaurants, parking garages, and shops merely located in government buildings;
- campaign offices and political-party organizations;
- duplicate or spoofed listings until resolved.

## Authenticity is a separate label

This codebook classifies the place, not the truth of individual reviews. Do not
exclude a government listing because its reviews look political, coordinated, or
implausible. Those are review-level measurement questions.

## Pilot validation protocol

1. Freeze the automated linkage version and the unique `gmap_id` input frame.
2. Draw separate probability samples of confirmed official candidates, uncertain
   official matches, keyword-only candidates, and general Google negatives.
3. Give two coders independently randomized files that hide automated status,
   scores, official names, and sampling strata.
4. Code `public_sector_label`, `service_family`, `public_facing`,
   `analytic_tier`, and `exclusion_reason`.
5. Revise definitions before national classification if Cohen's kappa is below
   0.70 for public-sector status or analytic tier.
6. Report stratum-weighted precision, recall, F1, and confusion matrices. The
   pilot target is at least 0.90 precision for
   `confirmed_official_candidate` and at least 0.70 F1 for each retained
   analytic tier. These are decision thresholds, not claims of universal truth.
7. Preserve both coder files and an adjudicated crosswalk; never overwrite the
   automated candidate label.
